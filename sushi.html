Sushi in Niigata City — where the fish and the rice are both local

Niigata is one of Japan's great rice regions, and even Japanese visitors from elsewhere say Niigata rice tastes different. Niigata City, the prefecture's largest city, is where the prefecture's best food gathers — and nowhere is that clearer than at a sushi counter. Off this coast, warm and cold currents meet, and the fish raised in rough water — nodoguro (blackthroat seaperch), winter yellowtail, nanban-ebi sweet shrimp — are firm and rich. Put them on shari made from Niigata rice and you have the combination local chefs are proudest of: top-grade fish on top-grade rice.

There is even a shared menu that proves it. The prefecture's sushi association created "Kiwami": ten pieces of the day's local fish, plus toro, uni and ikura, with a bowl of soup — ¥4,950 at around 50 member shops across the prefecture, each shop preparing it its own way. Four of the restaurants below serve it. It is the easiest first taste of Niigata sushi.

Prices below are guides from the sources listed, checked in November 2026. Menus, prices and hours change with the catch and the season — confirm when you book. Several of the counters below are reservation-only and book out early.

The omakase counters

Kyodai Zushi 兄弟寿し

The standard-bearer of "Niigatamae" — sushi that expresses Niigata rather than copying Tokyo's Edomae. The second-generation chef builds his omakase only from local ingredients: tuna landed on Sado, nanban-ebi, and shari made from Emi no Kizuna, a rice variety bred for sushi, cooked in spring water from Mt Myoko. Tabelog Award 2026 Silver; one of Tabelog's Top 100 sushi restaurants in eastern Japan (2025).

Furumachi / Nabeyacho-dori, Chuo-ku · Dinner only, omakase only · Guide price ¥20,000–29,999 · Closed Sundays · Booking essential

Tokiwa Zushi — Niigata branch 登喜和鮨 新潟店

The Niigata City branch (opened 2024) of a Shibata institution founded in 1954. Third-generation chef Kobayashi Kosuke uses almost nothing from outside Niigata — about 99% of ingredients, seasonings included, are prefectural; he serves no uni, because uni is not caught here. The shari is cooked in a traditional hagama pot. Winner of the Niigata Gastronomy Award 2026 Diamond Grand Prix; Tabelog Award 2026 Bronze. Bookable in English through the OMAKASE reservation platform.

Honcho-dori, Furumachi, Chuo-ku · Full course ¥28,600 · Nigiri-only lunch (Thu & Fri) from ¥11,000 · Weekdays in Niigata City (the chef is in Shibata at weekends) · Official website

Sushi Hatakeyama 鮨はたけやま

A seven-seat counter on Furumachi-dori, reservation-only, serving a single omakase course (¥16,500, about fifteen items). It took Silver at the Niigata Gastronomy Award 2026 — the same awards at which Tokiwa took the grand prix — and is part of the new generation of small counters remaking Furumachi sushi.

Furumachi-dori 9, Chuo-ku · Omakase course ¥16,500 · Closed Mondays · Official website

Sushi Nakaku Hoshino 鮨 奈可久 星野

Edomae technique applied to fish from the nearby sea, with ageing used to deepen flavour before the fish meets the rice. A small dinner-only room on Nishibori-dori; the guide price is around ¥17,000.

Nishibori-dori 5, Chuo-ku · Dinner only · Guide price ¥17,000 · Closed Sundays & holidays · Official page (Gurunavi)

Hatsune Zushi — main shop はつね寿司 本店

A six-seat counter on Kamiookawa-mae-dori, reservation-only and evenings only, in the same price bracket as Kyodai. One of Tabelog's Top 100 sushi restaurants in eastern Japan in 2021 and 2022.

Kamiookawa-mae-dori 7, Chuo-ku · Guide price ¥20,000–29,999 · Closed Wednesdays, Sundays & irregular days · Tabelog

Sushi Kurogo すし 黒衣

Furumachi's newest counter, opened in October 2025 on the second floor of a Higashibori-dori building. Chef Nagashima Naoto trained at Sushi Washoku Nagashima in Shibata. Two courses — nigiri omakase (¥7,700) or small dishes and nigiri (¥11,000) — make it the most accessible counter on this page; Bronze at the Niigata Gastronomy Award 2026.

Higashibori-dori 9, Chuo-ku (2F) · Courses ¥7,700 / ¥11,000 · Closed Sundays & holidays · Instagram

Furumachi classics

Marui 鮨・割烹 丸伊

Fifty years on Nabeyacho-dori, in the old geisha district. More than 30 kinds of fish from Niigata and Sado waters pass over the single-plank cypress counter; the house buys whole fish and uses each part at its best. Serves the "Kiwami" set (¥4,950), and has tatami rooms as well as the counter, so it works for groups and families where the two counters above do not.

Higashibori-dori, Chuo-ku · Lunch from around ¥3,000 · Kiwami set ¥4,950 · Official website

Minato Sushi 港すし

A Furumachi institution since the early Showa era, now in its third generation. The owner's rule is simple and is the theme of this page: "We use Niigata rice and Niigata fish." Visitors from outside the prefecture tell him Niigata's flavour is a gentle one — this is the place to test that.

Furumachi-dori 9, Chuo-ku · Official website

Sushi Benkei Kai — Furumachi 鮨 弁慶 海 古町店

The chef's-counter sibling of the Pier Bandai conveyor-belt shop below, produced by Edomae chef Yamazaki Masao: Sado ingredients worked with Tokyo technique. Lunch from ¥2,000, a nigiri course at ¥5,000, omakase up to ¥12,000.

Furumachi-dori 9, Chuo-ku · Lunch from ¥2,000 · Closed Sundays & holidays · Official website

Sushi Yasu 寿司安

Founded in 1934 as a street stall in Nishibori, and still uncompromising: only wild, seasonal fish — nothing farmed, nothing frozen. Eight counter seats, a regular in Tabelog's Top 100, and it serves the "Kiwami" set (¥4,950) as well as fuller courses.

Honcho-dori 4, Furumachi, Chuo-ku · Kiwami set ¥4,950 · Guide price ¥8,000–9,999 (dinner) · Closed Wednesdays & Thursdays · Official website

Daifuku Zushi 大福寿司

A sushi-kappo on Italia-koji, the little lane by the Hotel Italia-ken, built around fish from Sado waters. Its courses are named for the island and the city — Sado (¥8,800), Ryuto (¥11,000) and Daifuku (¥16,500).

Nishibori-dori 7 (Italia-koji), Chuo-ku · Courses from ¥8,800 · Closed Sundays & holidays · Official website

Sushi Maruyoshi 鮨 まる嘉

At the far end of Italia-koji, marked by an orange noren curtain. A seven-seat counter plus tatami rooms, with nigiri sets and courses made to your budget — a relaxed, old-school Furumachi room.

Nishibori-dori 7 (Italia-koji), Chuo-ku · Lunch sets in the ¥1,000s · Irregular holidays · Official website

Okina Zushi 翁寿し

A family-run classic on Nishibori-mae-dori. The lunch raw-nigiri set is ¥1,200, and the kitchen stays open until midnight — the everyday end of Furumachi sushi, done properly for decades.

Nishibori-mae-dori 6, Chuo-ku · Lunch set ¥1,200 · Closed 2nd & 4th Sundays · Tabelog

Shinbori Sushi 新堀寿司

Dinner only, and open until 1 a.m. — the late shift of Higashibori-dori, right next door to Kyodai. Around thirty kinds of fish are on hand at any time.

Higashibori-dori 8, Chuo-ku · Guide price ¥8,000–9,999 · Closed Sundays & holidays · Tabelog

Near the station — and Nuttari

Sushi Iwa すしいわ

Five minutes from the station. The chef, independent after 25 years at one of Niigata's best-known sushi houses, is particular about the shari above all: top-grade Iwafune Koshihikari, the prefecture's most prized rice brand, seasoned to draw out the fish caught off the Niigata coast.

Higashi-Odori, Chuo-ku · 5 min from Niigata Station · Official website

Sushi Senryu 寿司 川柳

A small hideaway down a back street, five minutes from the station. Local fish landed that day, the "Kiwami" set (¥4,950), and a sake list that runs to Niigata labels you rarely see outside the prefecture — fish and sake, both in season.

Station front, Chuo-ku · Kiwami set ¥4,950

Sekai Zushi せかい鮨

In Nuttari, the old market district east of the station, Sekai has been serving sushi for about ninety years. Its chef, Yoshizawa, heads the prefectural sushi association and devised the "Kiwami" set itself; the shop is also the birthplace of the seared nodoguro rice bowl (¥3,410), now a Niigata signature. The shari is Koshihikari from Tsugawa in Aga Town. Hours are short and vary by day — check before you go.

Nuttari Higashi 4, Chuo-ku · Kiwami set ¥4,950 · Nodoguro rice bowl ¥3,410 · Closed Mondays & Tuesdays · Official website

Casual & conveyor-belt

Sado Kaiten Sushi Benkei — Pier Bandai 佐渡 廻転寿司 弁慶 ピア万代店

Inside Pier Bandai, the city's food market, this is the Niigata City branch of Sado's famous Benkei. Fish caught off Sado arrives around noon and is prepared the same day (come after about 1 p.m. for the freshest selection); the shari is 100% Sado-grown Koshihikari. Plates from ¥176 — the full Niigata combination, local fish on local rice, at conveyor-belt prices. Named in Tabelog's Top 100 sushi restaurants.

Pier Bandai, Bandaijima, Chuo-ku · Plates ¥176–880 · Closed Wednesdays · Official website

Tomizushi — Niigata Station 富寿し 新潟駅前店

The station-front branch of Tomizushi, a conveyor-belt group from Joetsu, one minute from the Bandai exit. Local fish from the Joetsu coast at around ¥1,000–3,000 a visit — the easiest sushi stop in the city, straight off the Shinkansen.

Higashi-Odori 1, Chuo-ku · 1 min from Niigata Station · Guide price ¥1,000–3,000 · Closed Tuesdays · Official website

Sushi-dokoro Tengu 寿し処 天狗

Furumachi's late-night sushi bar: open until 3:30 a.m. (4 a.m. on Fridays and Saturdays), long after the counters have closed. Proper nigiri at izakaya hours, for ¥3,000–3,999 or so.

Furumachi-dori 9, Chuo-ku · Guide price ¥3,000–3,999 · Closed Sundays · Tabelog
