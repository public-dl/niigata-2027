Furumachi: one hundred places to eat and drink

Furumachi, in Niigata City, is where Niigata's sake lovers gather. In the Edo period it was counted among Japan's three great geisha districts, named alongside Shinbashi in Tokyo and Gion in Kyoto — and if you are lucky, walking these streets today you may still pass a geigi, a geisha in full kimono, on her way to an evening engagement. High-end ryotei and humble drinking spots stand side by side along the same few streets, a short trip from Toki Messe, the hall that hosts the Niigata Sake no Jin festival each March.

Sake here is chosen to match the dish, as wine is — and the food that goes with it has its own names, tsumami or ate: small plates meant to be enjoyed slowly, together with the cup. This page lists one hundred places in and around Furumachi where that culture lives: kappo counters and izakaya, sushi shops, bars open until dawn, soba for the road home, and the French and Italian restaurants scattered through the same streets.

The author's own recommendations are places he has actually visited. The rest are listed as a factual directory, compiled from official sites, Tabelog and local guides in November 2026. Hours are a guide; check before you go. There is also a map of all 100.

The author's own picks — places he has actually been

  • Sake to Meshi Kokon
    酒と飯 古今 — More than 20 sake labels from across Japan, all ¥500 a glass, with carefully made creative dishes. A grown-up sake bar.
  • Niigata Furumachi Jikon
    新潟古町 而今 — Kappo-level Japanese cooking with an izakaya's ease, built on Niigata ingredients.
  • Furumachi Saitō
    古町さいとう — Reservation only, eight seats, a single omakase course of many small tsumami dishes. A drinker's restaurant.
  • Bar FARO
    Bar FARO — The bar for a final drink.
  • Ryūto Furumachi Shunsai Suzuya
    柳都古町 旬菜 すゞ家 — And the first place to send a visitor: a former geisha house turned restaurant, serving Niigata's local dishes and jizake in the old hanamachi atmosphere.

And if money is no object

  • Ryōtei Nabedyaya
    料亭 鍋茶屋 — Founded in 1846; a registered cultural property at the heart of the geisha district, where geigi can still be called to your room.
  • Nihon Ryōri Yukihana
    日本料理 雪花 — Monthly-changing kaiseki built on Niigata's seasons, up to A5 Murakami beef.
  • Nihon Ryōri Ikinariya
    日本料理 行形亭 — Nearly 300 years old; kaiseki served in detached rooms across a 6,600 m² garden.
  • Kyodai Zushi
    兄弟寿し — Omakase only, “Niigata-mae” sushi from local ingredients alone (see the sushi feature).
  • Tokiwa Zushi (Niigata)
    登喜和鮨 新潟店 — The Niigata branch of Shibata's celebrated shop; 99% Niigata ingredients, down to the seasonings.

To finish: shime-soba

Niigata has a custom of finishing a night's drinking with soba — a shime, an epilogue in noodles. Two shops keep the custom alive deep into the night:

  • Kumaji (Magoshirō Soba)
    くまじ（孫四郎そば） — Open from 7:30 p.m. until dawn; the classic way to end the night, with the old shop's hegisoba.
  • Nishibori Soba
    西堀そば — Opened in spring 2026; hand-made nihachi soba and soba-mae snacks until 2:30 a.m.
