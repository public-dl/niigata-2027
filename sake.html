Niigata's sake breweries — the complete list

Niigata has more sake breweries than any other prefecture in Japan — 88 by the count usually cited, and the Niigata Sake Brewers Association's own directory currently lists 92, including new micro-breweries founded in the last few years. Until now there has been no single English list of them all: the association's detailed brewery pages are Japanese-only. This page is that list — and there is also a map of all the breweries.

Every brewery below is in the association's directory (as of 23 November 2026), grouped by region. English names and brands are cross-checked against the National Tax Agency's English sake brewery map; founding years and tour availability are as the association lists them. "Tours: Yes" means the brewery accepts visitors in some form — most require booking, and details change, so check the brewery's own site before you go.

Short on time? Start with these.
  • Imayo Tsukasa (Niigata City) — brewery tours with an English guide, a short walk from the old Nuttari fermentation district.
  • Ponshukan (Niigata, Nagaoka and Echigo-Yuzawa stations) — not a brewery, but the tasting room where you can try sake from across the whole prefecture, one small cup at a time.
  • Yoshinogawa (Nagaoka, founded 1548) — Niigata's oldest brewery, with its own sake museum in the Settaya brewing district.
  • Asahi Shuzo (Nagaoka) — maker of Kubota; free short brewery tours year-round.
  • Shirataki (Yuzawa) — beside Echigo-Yuzawa Station; the easiest brewery visit on a ski trip.
  • Obata — Gakkogura (Sado Island) — a working brewery inside a closed school, where visitors can join the brewing.
